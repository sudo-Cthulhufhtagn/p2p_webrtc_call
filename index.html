How the call gets made
1 · The introduction
The room code is an address on a public PeerJS broker. Both browsers say hello there and swap the details they need to find each other: candidate IP addresses and one encryption fingerprint each. That's the broker's entire job.
2 · The direct line
WebRTC then punches a path straight between the two machines and the broker drops out. Audio and video travel device to device — nothing in this project stores, records or relays a frame.
3 · The encryption
WebRTC has no unencrypted mode. Every stream is DTLS-SRTP, keys negotiated between the two browsers, so anyone tapping the wire — your ISP, the café wifi, the broker itself — sees ciphertext and packet timing, not your face or your voice.

The honest caveat. You verify each other's keys by trusting the broker that passed them along. A hostile broker could in principle hand both sides its own fingerprint and sit in the middle. Good enough for talking to friends; not an audited end-to-end system, so don't run a source meeting on it.
